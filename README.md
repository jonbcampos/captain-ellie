# Captain Ellie and the Space Raccoons

Ellie sits in a cardboard box in the backyard with a colander on her head. She taps **BLAST OFF!**,
and her imagination kicks in: the box turns into a shiny pink rocket and the backyard turns into
outer space. The cheeky Raccoon Bandits are up there in flying saucers, and Captain Ellie has to
chase them off.

She drags anywhere on the screen to fly up and down. The rocket fires on its own the whole time.

Nobody is hurt. A popped raccoon ship goes poof into a burst of stars, and the raccoon floats away
in a bubble helmet, waving, sticking his tongue out or blowing a raspberry. The raccoons tease her
("Nyah nyah!", "Missed me!") and toot slow, wobbly **stink bubbles** at her. She teases back.

| Thing | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Saucer** | drifts in on a gentle wave; sometimes sticks its tongue out |
| **Zipper** | a raccoon riding a skinny rocket, fast and straight |
| **Tooter** | a tin-can ship with a trumpet; parks on the right and toots stink bubbles |
| **Gift saucer** | wears a ribbon bow and glows; always drops a power-up |
| **Blue orb** | Super Laser: two lasers instead of one |
| **Yellow orb** | Star Power: lasers plus a fan of spinning stars |
| **Heart** | one more shield (up to four) |
| **Bomb** | MEGA BOOM: a ring that sweeps the whole screen clear |

Five stages, each about a minute of waves and then a silly boss with a health bar: **Captain
Saucer**, **Robo-Raccoon**, **Pirate Pete**, **Trash Can Tex** and **The Raccoon King**. Each
boss fight runs about 40-70 seconds in three phases: at two thirds and one third of its health the
boss gets MAD, glows red and attacks faster. On top of the spreads and aimed toots it charges along
her row ("LOOK OUT!"), throws a wall of bubbles with one gap, rains bubbles from above and bursts
a ring of them. A gift saucer flies in now and then during the fight. A boss goes out in a chain of about fifteen explosions, a slow-motion beat, a white flash and a MEGA
BOOM, because Ellie loves the booms more than anything.

**Space itself fights back.** Big asteroids drift in and take a lot of shooting to break (then
burst into rocks). Black holes pull the ship towards them and bend the stink bubbles round.
Giant comets flash a red warning over most of the screen ("BIG COMET! GO DOWN!") and then roar
through it.

**Two worlds, ten stages.** World 2, Mama's Galaxy, is where the Raccoon King runs to tell his
mom: bouncing snowballs, volcano eruptions, floating mines, asteroid walls, and Mama Raccoon in
her teapot mothership.

**Quiet help.** Every time she loses a stage, the next try drops more hearts, and after a couple
of losses she starts with an extra shield. Clearing the stage resets it.

**She has to fly the ship.** Every few seconds a red arrow flashes at the right edge and an
orange comet streaks in at whatever height she is. Lasers go straight through comets, so the only
way past is to slide out of the way. Stink bubbles and boss spreads aim at her too.

Getting hit pops a shield and gives two seconds of safety. With no shields left the rocket goes
KABOOM, then "Oopsie!" and **Try again!**, which restarts from the halfway checkpoint (or from the
boss, once she's reached it) with full shields but back to the single laser. Stars are for how few hits she took: three for one
hit or none, two for up to four.

Sixth in a set with [Flappy Unicorn](https://jonbcampos.github.io/flappy-unicorn/),
[Ellie's Rainbow Run](https://jonbcampos.github.io/runner-game/),
[Unicorn Squeeze Squad](https://jonbcampos.github.io/tower-defense/) and
[Ellie's Slingshot](https://jonbcampos.github.io/angry-birds/). It's built the same way:
TypeScript, a 2D canvas, no engine and no runtime dependencies.

## Art

The backyard title, Ellie's rocket, the raccoon ships and pilots, the five bosses, the power-ups
and the five space backdrops are painted by Google Gemini from the prompts in
`scripts/art-manifest.mjs`. The explosions and the stink clouds are slingshot's, copied over so a
BOOM looks the same in both games. All art is optional: delete `public/sprites/` and every piece
falls back to a hand-drawn version.

```bash
cp .env.example .env.local    # then put a Gemini API key in it (it's gitignored)
npm run art                   # generates whatever is missing; --only=id to redo one piece
npm run art:shrink            # resample to the sizes the game draws at
```

Then `__game.checkArt()` in the browser console confirms every sheet has the grid the slicer
expects.

## Sound

Voices and music are generated by Gemini (`npm run sound`, from `scripts/sound-manifest.mjs`):
cartoon lines for Ellie, the raccoons and the bosses, and Lyria music for the backyard, space and
the boss fights. Every effect (lasers, pops, toots, booms) is synthesised in `src/core/audio.ts`
and `src/core/sfx.ts`.

Ellie's lines are text-to-speech for now. Her real recordings go in as `public/sounds/e.<id>.m4a`
(extra takes as `e.<id>.2.m4a` and up; the game picks one at random). Then set `recorded: true` on
`ELLIE` in the sound manifest so a regenerate leaves them alone. Each of her lines in the manifest
has a `cue`, the situation to give her while recording.

## Running it

```bash
npm install && npm run dev
```

Open the printed Network URL on a phone. Hold it either way, because the game rotates itself. On
desktop, drag with the mouse or use the arrow keys (or W/S). `Esc` pauses, `R` restarts the stage,
and `Enter` or `Space` presses the big button.

## Why it's built this way

[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) records what was decided and why. The short version:

- **`src/game/`** is the simulation, in virtual pixels. It never imports from `src/render/`, and it
  reports what happened through an event queue that `main.ts` drains into particles, voices and
  sound.
- **`src/game/config.ts`** holds every tuning number. The stage scripts in `stages.ts` are the
  exception, a data table written with little formation helpers (`line`, `column`, `vee`, `gift`).
- Shots, enemies, bubbles, pickups, events and particles all live in fixed pools.
- **`src/core/`** is shared with the siblings: the fixed-step loop, the viewport that rotates
  itself in portrait, input, save, wake lock and the audio engine.

### Verifying it

```js
__game.verify()   // design contracts: spawns in the band, gifts in every stage, ship stays in
                  // the band, every boss falls to the base laser in under 45s, every stage
                  // reaches its boss
```

Every check runs on its own `GameState` and never touches the save.

`__game.stage(n)` jumps into a stage, `__game.boss()` skips to the current stage's boss,
`__game.advance(seconds)` runs the whole loop by hand, `__game.invincible()` turns off damage,
`__game.snap()` pins the current frame as an image, and `__game.unlockAll()` opens every stage.

## Status

Playable locally. Not yet a GitHub repo or deployed. Once it is, every push to `main` builds and
deploys to GitHub Pages at `/captain-ellie/` (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). It installs to a
phone's home screen and plays offline after the first visit. Bump `VERSION` in `public/sw.js` to
force installed copies to drop their cache.

The home-screen icon is drawn by `node scripts/make-icons.mjs`: a raccoon in a bubble helmet,
mid-raspberry, against the stars.
